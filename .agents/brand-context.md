# Brand context

## Brand

- **Name:** Clibble.
- **Category:** Independent terminal-first Pokemon catching adventure.
- **Description:** Explore Pokemon locations, catch creatures, and keep a collection from your terminal; browse saved creatures in a local web view.
- **Stage:** Released CLI, renamed from Dexrove to Clibble.
- **Owner:** Abdulrahman M. Yaqyn.

## Audience

- Primary audience: Pokemon fans comfortable with a terminal, and developers who enjoy inspectable local tools. This is a positioning hypothesis, not user-research evidence.
- Task: discover locations, build a collection, and return to saved stats.
- Language: explore, catch, inspect, collection, field guide.

## Positioning

- A focused local tool with no account, a persistent collection, and readable creature records.
- Niche, accessible, free/open-source. No claims of popularity or superiority.
- Alternatives include generic Pokédex viewers and existing command-line PokéAPI clients; no formal competitor study has been performed.

## Personality and voice

Playful, curious, friendly, clear. Use clear actions and brief recovery hints.
Clibble is pronounced “clib-ul”: a small, playful name with CLI tucked inside.
The name was selected by the owner after rejecting tool-like and forced adventure names.
The tagline is “A little wild in your terminal.” Avoid technical internals in user-facing status.

## Values and mission

Ownership of local data, reliable saves, readable interfaces, and honest scope.
Make discovering and catching Pokemon feel like a small adventure at the prompt.
Use warm character art for brand imagery. Interactive play uses a full-window RPG
screen with arrow/Enter navigation and second-person narration; `-plain` retains
the command prompt. Keep actual output readable and concise.
The cover is a conceptual title screen, not a promise of a navigable ASCII world.

## Goals

Deliver a coherent, memorable app identity and a dependable first-use experience.
Success signals are verified install/run paths, saved progress, clear recovery,
and consistent CLI, web, documentation, and release presentation.
